## Top 4, in submission order

### 1. Dr Laura Vuillier — the only genuine BPD route

- **Why first:** her offered project (secondary analysis of a large dataset on emotional functioning) is the **only project on the list where the student explicitly chooses the clinical population, with BPD named as an option**. With a BPD-first priority, no one else offers this.
- **Verified publications:** *Early evaluation of a DBT-informed online intervention for people with eating disorders* (Journal of Eating Disorders, 2024 — the intervention targeted alexithymia, emotion-regulation difficulties and beliefs about emotions, all with medium–large effects); *Striving for the perfect diet? The mediating role of perfectionism…* (2024); *Emotional overload in Bulimia Nervosa: an ERP study* (2025); *Believing emotions are uncontrollable is linked to eating disorder psychopathology via suppression and reappraisal* (2021).
- **Fit with main question:** she is the only supervisor with published work on **DBT-informed intervention**, **perfectionism as a mediator**, and **emotion regulation in clinical populations** combined.
- **Gap/risk:** no published work on dissociation or impulsivity; the dataset almost certainly has no longitudinal DBT outcomes, so expect the cross-sectional version of your question. She is likely popular (allocation risk), but that is what preferences 2–4 are for.
- **One-line pitch (already effectively sent in your email):** dissociation, impulsivity and perfectionism predicting emotion-regulation difficulties and beliefs about emotions in people high in BPD traits, as a secondary analysis of her emotional-functioning dataset.

### 2. Dr Alessio Bellato — BPD-capable via emotion dysregulation, plus your ADHD priority

- **Why second:** his Project 1, *Arousal and emotional dysregulation across mental disorders: a meta-analysis*, is transdiagnostic — a student can centre it on **emotional dysregulation in BPD** (arousal/psychophysiology is directly relevant to the dissociation-as-weakened-learning hypothesis in your question). He also covers your ADHD priority outright.
- **Verified evidence:** Southampton/Nottingham profiles confirm emotion dysregulation + heart-rate-variability psychophysiology as his core programme, including **autonomic correlates of emotional dysregulation and self-harm in young people** (self-harm is as BPD-adjacent as this list gets) and **predictive models of ADHD treatment response** — the same "who does treatment work for" logic as your DBT question.
- **Gap/risk:** students must pick one of his **three fixed projects** — least flexible on the list; none is BPD-specific, so the BPD angle depends on him agreeing to focus Project 1. Likely popular.
- **One-line pitch:** a meta-analysis of arousal/autonomic dysregulation in BPD (vs other disorders), framed as groundwork for understanding why emotion-focused therapies fail for high-dissociation patients.

### 3. Dr Hafiz Muhammad Shakeel — your main question's *logic*, applied to ADHD

- **Why third:** his entire programme is **predicting individual treatment response from pre-treatment characteristics** (the CARE in ADHD project: predicting who benefits from methylphenidate from clinical, cognitive and physiological baseline data). That is structurally identical to your main question — baseline traits predicting therapy outcome — swapped onto ADHD, your second priority. It also serves your "impulsive thoughts and inattention measured with advanced technology" interest.
- **Verified evidence:** Southampton profile and the CARE in ADHD project page confirm treatment/drug-response prediction modelling, explainable ML, and digital triage with NHS partners.
- **Gap/risk:** no BPD work at all, and the methods are ML-heavy — check you're comfortable with that. **Allocation advantage:** he is newer to the department and was not profiled with set projects; newer staff are exactly where the department places over-subscribed students, so he is both a genuine fit and a realistic get.
- **One-line pitch:** baseline impulsivity/inattention profiles as predictors of treatment response or dropout in ADHD, using interpretable prediction models.

### 4. Dr Yidian Gao — the impulsivity specialist

- **Why fourth:** of everyone on the list, Gao has the strongest publication record on **impulsivity** — a core construct of both your BPD question and your ADHD interests. Existing neuroimaging datasets on *conduct problems, impulsivity and developmental risk* mean a feasible secondary-analysis MSc.
- **Verified publications:** *Cortical structure and subcortical volumes in conduct disorder: a coordinated analysis of 15 international cohorts* (The Lancet Psychiatry, 2024, first author); *Dimensional Associations Between Conduct Problems and Brain Structure Across 18 International Cohorts in ENIGMA* (JAACAP, 2026); earlier work on brain architecture and psychopathic traits in adolescents with conduct disorder (2017).
- **Gap/risk:** populations are youth with conduct problems/maltreatment, not BPD — though early maltreatment and impulsivity are the developmental pathway *into* BPD, which is a defensible framing. Neuroimaging analysis has a learning curve.
- **One-line pitch:** impulsivity dimensions and brain structure/connectivity in youth at developmental risk, framed as precursors of borderline-spectrum emotional dysregulation.

## Feasibility: three realistic versions of your question

1. **Systematic review / meta-analysis** of baseline dissociation, impulsivity and perfectionism as predictors of DBT (or broader psychotherapy) dropout and non-response in BPD. Supportable by: Vuillier, Bellato (his Project 1 format), Morriss (offers reviews).
2. **Secondary data analysis**: those traits predicting emotion-regulation difficulties and beliefs about emotions in people high in BPD traits. Supportable by: Vuillier (best — her dataset, BPD population choice), Brignell (her datasets, non-BPD).
3. **Prediction-modelling study**: baseline traits predicting treatment response/dropout, ADHD population. Supportable by: Shakeel, Bellato.
